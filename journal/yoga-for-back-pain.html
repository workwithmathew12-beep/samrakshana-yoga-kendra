Yoga for lower back pain: a gentle, practical approach

Why slow, guided yoga suits stiff and sore backs, which kinds of movement tend to help, and when to check with your doctor first. From Samrakshana Yoga Kendra, Chennai.

Samrakshana Yoga KendraR.A. Puram, Chennai4 min read21 October 2026

Lower back pain is one of the most common reasons people come to yoga. Long hours at a desk, driving, lifting and simply getting older all load the spine. Done carefully, yoga can help many people move more comfortably and feel more in control of their backs.

Why a gentle approach matters

A sore back needs movement, but not strain. The aim is to restore easy, pain-free movement step by step: mobility first, then gradual strength, with the breath guiding the pace. Pushing into deep stretches too early is a common mistake.

Movements that often help

  • Breath-led spinal movement, such as gently arching and rounding the back on hands and knees
  • Supported forward rests, like child's pose with a bolster or blocks
  • Hip mobility, because tight hips often pull on the lower back
  • Core and back strength, built slowly and without holding the breath
  • Relaxation, because pain and tension feed each other

Which of these suit you depends on your back. That is why a short assessment before practice is worth it.

When to see your doctor first

Please check with a doctor before starting if your pain followed a fall or accident, travels down the leg with numbness or weakness, is getting steadily worse, or comes with fever or unexplained weight loss. Yoga therapy works alongside medical advice, never instead of it.

Quick answers

Can yoga make back pain worse?

It can if movements are forced or unsuitable. Guided, gradual practice adapted to your back reduces that risk.

How often should I practise?

Short, regular practice usually helps more than occasional long sessions. Your teacher will suggest a routine that suits you.
