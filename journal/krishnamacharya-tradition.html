The Krishnamacharya tradition: why yoga should fit the person

The Chennai-rooted idea at the heart of yoga therapy, and how it shapes every session at Samrakshana Yoga Kendra in R.A. Puram.

Samrakshana Yoga KendraR.A. Puram, Chennai3 min read21 October 2026

T. Krishnamacharya (1888–1989), often called the father of modern yoga, spent his later decades teaching in Chennai. His central idea was simple: yoga must be adapted to the individual, to their age, health, circumstances and goals.

Practice for the person in front of you

Rather than one fixed sequence for all, the teacher observes the student and builds a practice that suits them. Breath links every movement. Postures are chosen and modified with care. The practice evolves as the student does.

A living lineage in Chennai

Krishnamacharya's teaching continues through Krishnamacharya Yoga Mandiram in Chennai and through teachers he trained, such as Srivatsa Ramaswami, known for Vinyasa Krama.

How it shapes Samrakshana

R. Ramya completed her 400-hour teacher training at Krishnamacharya Yoga Mandiram and a 100-hour Vinyasa Krama course with Srivatsa Ramaswami's school. At Samrakshana Yoga Kendra, that shows up in small, practical ways: an unhurried first conversation, breath-led movement, and a practice that changes as you do.

Quick answers

What is Vinyasa Krama?

A breath-centred approach to sequencing postures, taught by Srivatsa Ramaswami, a long-time student of Krishnamacharya.

Is this style suitable for beginners?

Yes. Adapting practice to the individual makes it suitable for beginners and experienced students alike.
