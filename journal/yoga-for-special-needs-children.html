Yoga for children with special needs: what a session looks like

Structure, patience and play: how yoga is adapted for children and adults on the autism spectrum at Samrakshana Yoga Kendra, Chennai.

Samrakshana Yoga KendraR.A. Puram, Chennai3 min read21 October 2026

Yoga can give children with special needs a calm, predictable space to move, breathe and feel their bodies. The key is adapting the practice to the child, rather than asking the child to fit a class.

What a session can include

  • A predictable routine, so the child knows what comes next
  • Simple postures taught through imitation, stories and play
  • Breathing games that build awareness gently
  • Quiet time to settle at the end

Patience over perfection

Progress is measured in small steps: joining in for longer, following a sequence, trying something new. Parents are welcome to share what works at home, so the practice can build on it.

A teacher trained in special education

R. Ramya holds a Diploma in Special Education (Autism Spectrum Disorder) as well as her yoga therapy qualifications, and works with both children and adults with special needs. Message us to talk about your child before the first session.

Quick answers

Can my child join a regular class?

Some children thrive in groups and others need one-to-one sessions first. We decide together after meeting your child.

Should parents attend?

Often, especially at first. We will agree on what helps your child most.
