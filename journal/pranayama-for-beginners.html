Pranayama for beginners: five things to know before you start

What pranayama is, how breathing practice is taught in the Krishnamacharya tradition, and how to begin pranayama classes in Chennai.

Samrakshana Yoga KendraR.A. Puram, Chennai3 min read21 October 2026

Pranayama is the practice of working with the breath. In yoga it is considered as important as the postures, and for many people it is the part that changes their day the most.

1. It starts with noticing

Before any technique, you learn to notice how you breathe: fast or slow, high in the chest or low in the belly. Awareness comes first.

2. Longer exhales calm the body

Simple patterns in which the out-breath is a little longer than the in-breath are a common, gentle starting point for steadying the mind.

3. Comfort always wins

Breathing practice should never feel strained. If you feel dizzy, breathless or anxious, stop and breathe normally.

4. Some techniques need a teacher

Practices that involve holding the breath or forceful breathing are not for everyone, particularly people with high blood pressure, heart conditions or during pregnancy. Learn them with guidance.

5. Little and often

A few minutes every day does more than a long session once a week.

Learning pranayama at Samrakshana

In the Krishnamacharya tradition, breath links every movement, and pranayama is tailored to the individual. At Samrakshana Yoga Kendra in R.A. Puram, pranayama is taught in group sessions and one-to-one, alongside meditation and healing chants.

Quick answers

Can beginners do pranayama?

Yes. Gentle breath awareness and simple patterns are suitable for most beginners.

Is pranayama safe in pregnancy?

Some techniques are not. Practise only with a qualified teacher and your doctor’s approval.
